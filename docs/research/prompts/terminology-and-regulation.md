Research theme: standard terminology, regulatory framing and re-identification risk — for the vocabulary of the `nyms` API and docs, and for its sensitivity policy and entity taxonomy seam.

Cover: (1) The established definitions and the distinctions between pseudonymisation, anonymisation, de-identification, redaction, masking, tokenisation, surrogate generation; GDPR Art. 4(5) and Recital 26; EDPB Guidelines 01/2025 on pseudonymisation (adopted January 2025, what they say about "additional information", keyed transformations and the pseudonymisation domain); ISO/IEC 20889:2018 terminology; HIPAA Safe Harbor's 18 identifier classes and Expert Determination, as a reference taxonomy. (2) Re-identification risk: the concepts (singling out, linkability, inference; k-anonymity, l-diversity, t-closeness; motivated-intruder test, ICO anonymisation guidance), what they mean for free text rather than tables, and how a toolkit should report residual risk honestly. (3) Entity taxonomies used by tools (Presidio's entity list, Google DLP infoTypes, AWS Comprehend PII types, HIPAA, the EU "special categories" of Art. 9) and a recommended, extensible taxonomy with sensitivity tiers for `nyms`, distinguishing direct identifiers, quasi-identifiers, sensitive attributes (health, finance, legal, amounts, dates), and credentials. (4) Danish specifics: the CPR number (structure, the date part, the serial part and sex, the modulus-11 check and its abolition for numbers issued after 2007, the frequency of numbers failing it), CVR numbers (8 digits, modulus-11 weights), Danish address formats, phone numbers, bank account numbers (registreringsnummer + kontonummer), Danish IBAN, NemKonto/MitID and other identifiers a legal or financial document would carry; Datatilsynet (the Danish DPA) guidance on pseudonymisation and on using cloud AI services with personal data, if published. (5) The practical question of what a sensitivity "policy" is in existing tools (allow-lists, deny-lists, entity selection before vs after detection, score thresholds) and what the user-facing policy object should look like.
