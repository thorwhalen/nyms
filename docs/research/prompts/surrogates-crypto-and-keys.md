Research theme: surrogate (pseudonym) generation, the cryptography behind it, readable encodings, the mapping store and key management — for three seams of `nyms`: the surrogate generator, the reversible mapping store, and key management.

Cover: (1) Surrogate strategies and their properties (determinism, reversibility, format preservation, linkability across documents, dictionary-attack resistance, readability for a human and for an LLM): plain numbering (PERSON_1), user-chosen aliases, random tokens with a vault (tokenisation as in PCI DSS), keyed hashing (HMAC-SHA-256, BLAKE2b keyed mode, truncation and collision handling, the salt-vs-key distinction, why an unkeyed hash of a low-entropy identifier such as a CPR number is trivially reversible by enumeration), format-preserving encryption (NIST SP 800-38G FF1 and FF3-1, the FF3 break and revision, the `ff3` and `pyffx` Python libraries and their licences and maintenance), deterministic authenticated encryption (AES-SIV, RFC 5297; `cryptography`'s AESSIV), consistent fake-data surrogates (Faker seeded per entity), and the EDPB 01/2025 view of which of these count as pseudonymisation. (2) Readable encodings of a hash or number into words, with word-list sizes, bits per word, licences and Python availability: PGP word list, EFF diceware lists, BIP-39, proquints, Bubble Babble, humanhash, codenamize, petname/Docker-style names, what3words-style schemes, the mnemonic Major System (digits to consonant sounds), and syllable-based pronounceable generators (Koremutake, FIPS 181 APG-style); which are most robust when an LLM paraphrases text (the surrogate must survive round-tripping through a model and come back verbatim for re-identification), and how to make surrogates look like what they replace (a name-shaped surrogate for a name, a number-shaped one for an amount) without lying about it. (3) The mapping store: what it must record (entity type, original, surrogate, spans, document, time), encryption at rest (Fernet, AES-GCM, age), separating the key from the store, append-only logs, the consequences of losing the key or the store, and how `dol`-style key-value stores fit. (4) Key management for a single user or small team: key derivation (Argon2id, scrypt, HKDF), OS keyrings (the `keyring` package, macOS Keychain), `age`, envelope encryption, per-project vs per-document keys, rotation and re-keying of a mapping store, backup, and what NIST SP 800-57 and the EDPB say about keeping the "additional information" separate. (5) Give a recommended default for each seam and the replacement each should allow.
